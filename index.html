Research

Student Research Assistant, Freie Universität Berlin

May 2024 – present · Berlin · MSc thesis: Correlations in Non-Markovian Open Quantum System Dynamics (Prof. Christiane Koch)

  • Implemented and compared two methods for simulating a quantum system coupled to a bath with memory: chain mapping with matrix product states (TEBD) and the surrogate Hamiltonian.
  • Showed that the time a truncated simulation stays accurate grows linearly with chain length and does not depend on coupling strength. This gives a rule for choosing chain length for a target simulation time.
  • Mapped the trade-off: chain mapping was reliable across all regimes but needed about 50 modes, while the surrogate Hamiltonian covered a similar time window with 15 but only under strict sampling conditions.
  • Presented at the DPG annual conference.

Honours research, Monash University

2022 · First Class Honours · Slowly Decaying Correlation in a Simple Metastable Quantum System (Prof. Kavan Modi)

  • Built a small simulation in Python of a quantum system coupled to two environment qubits, and measured how long it remembers its past using multi-time correlations.
  • Found a hidden effect: the usual spectral test showed nothing, but a small random perturbation exposed a very slow-decaying (metastable) state.
  • Showed that memory fades as a power law, slower as the swap angle shrinks, and proposed a four-time correlation test that can be measured experimentally.
